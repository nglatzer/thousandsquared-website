How to prepare the monthly numbers

Count full-time employees using the ACA hours-of-service rules, generally 30 hours per week or 130 per month. For everyone not counted as full time, cap each person’s monthly hours at 120, then add those capped hours together. Do not include full-time employees’ hours in this total. Raw combined payroll hours cannot be capped accurately without employee-level data. Include paid hours of service where required and review applicable employee exclusions. IRS ALE guidance ↗

ACA company affiliation screening

Business Affiliation Checker

Check affiliation across 2–6 companies to see which employees to count together for ALE purposes. Choose a brother–sister or parent–subsidiary relationship and enter the ownership percentages.

Scope: this screen handles simple taxable corporations and partnerships with matching ownership interests. It checks the percentages held by the listed owners and companies on the selected date. It does not automatically adjust ownership for family relationships or other attribution rules, or evaluate changes over an entire year. Use Company A–F and Owner 1–10 as aliases. Owner names are optional and stay in this page’s memory; do not enter EINs or employee details.

Maximum 6 companies.

Try an example

Names or aliases help identify owners; relationships are for review, not automatic attribution. For each family relationship, identify the other owner in the same cell. Family relationships or uncertainty trigger professional review. Leave names blank if preferred; no information is saved or submitted by this calculator.

Enter known percentages to four decimals, including explicit 0 where there is no interest. Blank means unknown. Each company’s entered percentages must total no more than 100%. Include every potentially common individual owner in brother–sister mode, or every relevant holding company in parent mode. Unrelated outside interests may be omitted.

Which ownership percentage should I use?

Corporate tests consider voting power or share value; partnerships consider capital or profits. This simplified screen uses one percentage only where those interests match. If they differ, or the entity is disregarded, tax-exempt, an insurance company, a trust, an estate, or a sole proprietorship, mark the complex-structure box and seek review. Before relying on the screen

Choose a test and enter ownership percentages. The result will explain affiliation for ALE purposes and any review requirements.

How the tests work: brother–sister screening tests sets of up to five owners who each own an interest in every tested company. The same set must reach at least 80% in each company and more than 50% identical ownership, using each owner’s smallest interest across the tested companies. Parent screening adds companies when qualifying group entities together hold at least 80%. Chain percentages are not simply multiplied.

The result identifies the workforce to include for each company’s ALE determination from all entered passing ownership groups containing that company. Ownership classifications are shown in the supporting calculations. Overlapping relationships and circular ownership require review. Attribution can change effective ownership, and affiliated service groups can require aggregation without meeting these percentage tests. 1095-C code explainer

Understand every 1095-C code.

Look up coverage offers, enrollment, employment status and affordability safe harbors. Search a code such as 1E or 2C, or a topic such as ICHRA, COBRA or self-insured coverage.

Line 14 describes the offer. Line 16 describes enrollment, employment or relief. For example, 1E can describe an offer to the employee, spouse and dependents, while 2C confirms the employee’s enrollment for the entire month.

MEC means minimum essential coverage. MV means minimum value. A spouse is separate from dependents for these reporting codes. Report monthly facts; use “All 12 Months” only when the same entry applies throughout the year.

30 code explanations · Select a code to read more.

1AQualifying Offer

A full-time employee is offered MEC providing minimum value, with MEC also offered to the spouse and dependents. The employee’s self-only cost meets the tax year’s indexed mainland federal poverty line limit. Leave Line 15 blank for this code.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1BEmployee only

MEC providing minimum value is offered to the employee. Neither the spouse nor dependents receive an MEC offer.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1CEmployee and dependents

The employee is offered MEC providing minimum value, and dependents are offered MEC. The spouse is not offered MEC.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1DEmployee and spouse

The employee is offered MEC providing minimum value, and the spouse is offered MEC. Dependents are not offered MEC. Use 1J instead when the spouse’s offer is conditional.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1EEmployee, spouse and dependents

The employee is offered MEC providing minimum value, and both the spouse and dependents are offered MEC. Use 1K instead when the spouse’s offer is conditional.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1FMEC without minimum value

The offered coverage qualifies as MEC but does not provide minimum value. The offer may cover just the employee or also include family members.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1GSelf-insured enrollment: never full-time during the year

The individual enrolled in the employer’s self-insured coverage for at least one month and was either never an employee during the year or never a full-time employee in any month. Use 1G for all 12 months, even if enrollment lasted only part of the year. Leave Lines 15 and 16 blank; report actual covered months in Part III.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1HNo MEC offer

No coverage was offered, or the offered coverage was not MEC. This also generally covers months before employment or after termination. An offer must generally provide coverage for every day of the month; a partial-month offer does not qualify as a full-month offer.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1JEmployee and conditional spouse offer

The employee is offered MEC providing minimum value. The spouse’s MEC offer depends on a condition, such as having no coverage available through another employer. Dependents are not offered MEC.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1KEmployee, dependents and conditional spouse offer

The employee is offered MEC providing minimum value, dependents are offered MEC, and the spouse’s MEC offer is conditional.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1LICHRA: employee only · residence ZIP

An individual coverage HRA is offered only to the employee. Affordability uses the employee’s primary residence ZIP code.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1MICHRA: employee and dependents · residence ZIP

An individual coverage HRA is offered to the employee and dependents, excluding the spouse. Affordability uses the employee’s primary residence ZIP code.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1NICHRA: employee, spouse and dependents · residence ZIP

An individual coverage HRA is offered to the employee, spouse and dependents. Affordability uses the employee’s primary residence ZIP code.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1OICHRA: employee only · worksite ZIP

An individual coverage HRA is offered only to the employee. Affordability uses the primary employment site ZIP code under the location safe harbor.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1PICHRA: employee and dependents · worksite ZIP

An individual coverage HRA is offered to the employee and dependents, excluding the spouse. Affordability uses the primary employment site ZIP code under the location safe harbor.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1QICHRA: employee, spouse and dependents · worksite ZIP

An individual coverage HRA is offered to the employee, spouse and dependents. Affordability uses the primary employment site ZIP code under the location safe harbor.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1RICHRA: unaffordable offer

An individual coverage HRA is offered but is not affordable. The offer may be employee-only or include the spouse and/or dependents. Leave Line 15 blank for this code.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1SICHRA: individual not full-time

An individual coverage HRA is offered to someone who is not a full-time employee. Check the year-wide 1G rule separately when that person enrolled in self-insured coverage and was never full-time during the year. Leave Line 15 blank for 1S.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1TICHRA: employee and spouse · residence ZIP

An individual coverage HRA is offered to the employee and spouse, excluding dependents. Affordability uses the employee’s primary residence ZIP code.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1UICHRA: employee and spouse · worksite ZIP

An individual coverage HRA is offered to the employee and spouse, excluding dependents. Affordability uses the primary employment site ZIP code under the location safe harbor.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

1I, 1V, 1W, 1X, 1Y, 1ZReserved codes

These six Line 14 codes are reserved in the 2025 instructions. Do not use them for a current coverage scenario.

Form 1095-C · Line 14

2ANot employed at any time in the month

The person was not an employee on any day of the month. Do not use 2A in a month with even one day of employment, including the termination month. For later months of post-employment COBRA enrollment, use 2A rather than 2C.

Form 1095-C · Line 16

2BNot full-time, or qualifying termination month

The employee was not full-time for the month and did not enroll in offered MEC. This code also applies when a full-time employee’s offer or coverage ended before month-end solely because employment terminated, and would otherwise have continued through month-end.

Form 1095-C · Line 16

2CEnrolled in employer health coverage

The employee was enrolled in the employer’s MEC for every day of the month. This generally takes priority over other Line 16 codes, including affordability safe harbors. Exceptions: use 2E when multiemployer relief applies; do not use 2C with Line 14 code 1G, for non-MEC coverage, or for post-employment COBRA or other post-employment coverage. An offer or a waiver alone does not establish enrollment.

Form 1095-C · Line 16

2DLimited Non-Assessment Period

A qualifying section 4980H(b) period applies, such as an eligible initial measurement period or waiting period. A company’s waiting period does not automatically qualify: the measurement method, timing and subsequent offer requirements matter. Use 2D rather than 2B for a qualifying initial measurement period; 2E takes priority when applicable.

Form 1095-C · Line 16

2EMultiemployer interim relief

The employer qualifies for the multiemployer arrangement interim guidance for this employee and month. This code takes priority over other Line 16 codes, including 2C and 2D. A union employee or a contribution alone does not establish eligibility for the relief.

Form 1095-C · Line 16

2FAffordability: Form W-2 safe harbor

The employer uses the employee’s Form W-2 wages to establish affordability under the applicable safe harbor. If selected for that employee, this method must be used consistently for all months in the calendar year when coverage is offered, subject to the Line 16 ordering rules.

Form 1095-C · Line 16

2GAffordability: federal poverty line safe harbor

The employer establishes affordability using the applicable federal poverty line safe harbor for the month. This is a Line 16 affordability code; it is separate from the Line 14 Qualifying Offer code 1A.

Form 1095-C · Line 16

2HAffordability: rate of pay safe harbor

The employer establishes affordability using the applicable rate of pay safe harbor. The rules differ for hourly and salaried employees; use the required calculation rather than simply comparing the premium with actual take-home pay.

Form 1095-C · Line 16

2IReserved code

This Line 16 code is reserved in the 2025 instructions. Do not use it to report a current safe harbor or relief.

Form 1095-C · Line 16

Line 15: the employee’s required contribution

Report the monthly employee cost for the lowest-cost self-only MEC offer that provides minimum value. This may differ from the premium for the coverage the employee actually selected. If the required contribution is zero, enter 0.00.

Complete Line 15 with 1B, 1C, 1D, 1E, 1J, 1K, 1L, 1M, 1N, 1O, 1P, 1Q, 1T or 1U. Leave it blank for other Line 14 codes.

ICHRA: the amount generally reflects the applicable lowest-cost self-only silver plan premium minus the monthly ICHRA amount, using the applicable age and location rules. For 1L, 1M, 1N and 1T, Line 17 reports the residence ZIP; for 1O, 1P, 1Q and 1U, it reports the worksite ZIP used under the location safe harbor.

Common combinations and reporting rules

  • 1E + 2C: an MEC offer to the employee, spouse and dependents that provides minimum value for the employee, with the employee enrolled in MEC for every day of that month.
  • 1E + 2F: the same offer, with the employee declining coverage and the employer meeting the W-2 affordability safe harbor and other applicable reporting conditions.
  • 1H + 2A: no MEC offer and no employment on any day of the month.
  • 1H + 2D: no full-month offer during a qualifying Limited Non-Assessment Period.
  • 1G + blank Line 16: qualifying year-wide self-insured reporting for someone who was never full-time during the year.
  • Waived coverage: there is no separate waiver code. Enter the applicable Line 16 code, or leave the month blank when none applies.
  • Safe harbors 2F, 2G and 2H: apply the tax year’s indexed affordability percentage. Do not use these codes for months marked “No” in Form 1094-C, Part III, column (a), for the MEC offer indicator.
  • COBRA: a post-termination COBRA offer is generally not reported as an offer to a former employee; later nonemployment months generally use 1H and 2A. COBRA after reduced hours while still employed follows different offer and enrollment rules.
  • Self-insured coverage and ICHRA: Part III separately reports enrolled individuals and months of coverage. Unlike the full-month 2C rule, Part III can report coverage for a month with at least one covered day.
